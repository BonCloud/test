TURN CINEMATIC: your files
==========================

Everything in this folder can be replaced. Either overwrite the file with the
same name, or put yours anywhere and point the setting at it
(Configure Settings > Turn Cinematic). File names are case-sensitive on
Linux and hosted servers, so keep stamp-D.png with a capital D.


BACKGROUND VIDEOS AND FACTION SOUNDS  (you supply these, none are included)

  prophet.webm      prophet.mp3
  (angel)           angel.mp3
  liquidator.webm   liquidator.mp3
  survivor.webm     survivor.mp3

  Video: .webm. It plays muted and loops, so sound comes from the audio file.
  Audio: .mp3 or .ogg. Foundry v13 no longer accepts .webm in audio pickers.
  A missing video falls back to a plain colored band. A missing sound is silent.
  The Angel has no band or video (it floats straight over the game), so it
  only needs its sound.


THE ANGEL'S PICTURES  (placeholders are included; replace them with your art)

  angel-wing.png    A transparent PNG of the RIGHT wing.
                    Draw it with the shoulder (where it joins the body) on the
                    LEFT edge, vertically centered. The left wing is the same
                    picture flipped. If your art has two different wings, set
                    "Angel: left wing image" in settings and the left one will
                    be used as it is, not flipped.
                    Roughly 1000 x 1000 works well; the shape can be anything.

  angel-halo.png    A square transparent PNG, drawn centered. It turns slowly
                    behind the portrait. Higher grades stack more copies,
                    bigger and turning the other way.

  angel-hands.png   A transparent PNG of arms and hands reaching UP, cut off
                    flat along the bottom edge (that edge sits on the bottom
                    of the screen). Grades B and A reach in from the two bottom
                    corners, in front of everything except the stamp. They are mirrored
                    so they lean toward the middle; turn "lean toward the
                    middle" off in settings to use the picture as drawn.
                    If you leave it empty, the hands are simply left out.

  stamp-D.png       The grade stamps: square transparent PNGs, one per grade.
  stamp-C.png       They are thrown onto the screen under the
                    Angel's name, land with a thud and
  stamp-B.png       flung ink, then settle as a print. If a stamp file is
  stamp-A.png       missing, a plain drawn stamp with the grade letter is used.

  stamp.ogg         The sound of the stamp landing (.ogg or .mp3).

  If your art doesn't line up, you don't need to edit anything: use the
  "wing size", "wing distance from center", "wing height", "halo size",
  "reaching hands size" and "stamp size" sliders in the module settings.


THE PROPHET'S PICTURES  (placeholders are included; replace them with your art)

  The Prophet has two looks, chosen in settings under "Prophet: look":
  Biblical (the default, built from the three pictures below) and Hunted (the
  typed report with a reticle, which needs no pictures).

  prophet-seal.png       A square transparent PNG of the seal (the wax part),
                         drawn centered. The portrait is placed in the MIDDLE
                         of it, so leave a round space there, about 45% of the
                         picture's width. 1300 x 1300 is plenty. It is
                         slammed down first, then the rest follows.

  prophet-parchment.png  A WIDE transparent PNG of the strip or scroll, longer
                         than it is tall (the included one is 2400 x 440).
                         It lies behind the seal and unrolls from the seal
                         to the right, like the other factions' bands. The
                         picture is stretched to fit the strip, so any wide
                         shape works. Its LEFT end is hidden under the seal,
                         so put nothing important there; the RIGHT end is
                         the torn or rolled edge people see. The name is
                         written on it in dark ink: "PROPHET" small on top,
                         the name under it.

  prophet-rays.png       A square transparent PNG of rays of light, drawn
                         centered on the seal and behind everything. It is
                         drawn very large (about two and a half screens high),
                         so make it big, 2000 x 2000 or more.

  There is no background picture behind the Prophet: only a light, dark-purple
  tint over the game (turn it off with "Prophet: darken the screen behind"),
  so the game stays visible. The Prophet's video (prophet.webm) is not used
  by this look (the Hunted look still uses it). The seal sits toward the left
  of the screen and slams down first; then the strip unrolls from it to the
  right with the name on it. If a picture is missing it is simply left out;
  with no parchment the name turns light so it can still be read. Long names
  shrink to fit the strip.

  If your art doesn't line up, use the "Prophet seal size", "Prophet parchment
  thickness", "Prophet rays size" and "Prophet name height" sliders in
  settings. "Prophet position across the screen" moves the seal (and so the
  whole strip) left or right (17 is the default, 50 is the middle).

  If you replaced the old tall parchment with your own picture: it is now
  stretched along a wide strip, so a tall picture will look squashed. Use a
  wide one.

TURN ORDER (no files needed, all in Configure Settings > Turn Cinematic)

  Turn order: "Activation" (the default) replaces initiative. Each round the
  sides take turns, ENEMY FIRST, then a friendly, then an enemy, and so on
  until everyone has gone. If one side has more fighters than the other, the
  bigger side plays out the rest of its fighters, one after another, once the
  smaller side has had all of its turns.
    - Enemy = a token whose disposition is Hostile. Everyone else (friendly,
      neutral, and any character a player owns) is a friendly.
    - Nobody rolls initiative. The module writes numbers into the combat
      tracker so it shows the order, and the tracker's own Next Turn button
      just works. You can still drag people in the tracker; they keep their
      place within their side.
    - Defeated fighters go to the bottom and are skipped.
    - PICKING WHO GOES NEXT: in the combat tracker, a green play button
      appears on the right of a fighter's row when they can go next. When the
      enemy's turn comes up, only enemies who haven't gone yet have the
      button (never a friendly); when a friendly's turn comes up, only
      friendlies who haven't gone have it (never an enemy). Pressing it ends
      the current turn and starts that fighter's turn, with their cinematic.
      The fallen never have it. When the other side has nobody left, the
      button is on the rest of the side that is still standing.
      Press Next Turn instead and the top fighter of the side whose turn it
      is goes (so the Next Turn button still works on its own).
    - The GM can press the button on anyone who is up. A player sees it only
      on their own characters, and their press is carried out by the GM's
      computer, so the GM has to be logged in.
    - Whoever opens a round is the first enemy in the tracker: the round
      starts the moment the last turn of the old one ends. (Drag the enemies
      in the tracker to change who that is.)
    - The order is lined up the moment fighters are added to the combat
      tracker (you don't have to start the fight first), and again when
      someone joins or leaves, or a token changes sides. Fighters who have
      already gone this round never move.
    - The combat tracker shows it: the number on the right of each row is the
      activation order (not an initiative number), the row's edge is red for
      an enemy and blue for a friendly, fighters who have gone are dimmed,
      the fallen show a cross, and a line at the top says which side goes
      next. The "roll initiative" buttons are hidden, because nobody rolls.
    - Choose "Initiative" in the same setting to go back to Foundry's normal
      order and tracker; the kill cinematics and the ping still work there.

MAP PING (setting: "Ping the token whose turn it is")

  When a turn starts, once its cinematic has finished, the GM's computer
  pings that fighter's token on the map, so everyone can see who it is.
    - "Ping the token" (the default) is the normal pulsing ping. "Ping and
      pull" also moves everyone's view to the token. "Off" turns it off.
    - Only the GM's computer pings (Foundry shows it to everybody), so it is
      not repeated once per player. The GM has to be logged in.
    - Hidden fighters and hidden tokens are never pinged. A fighter with no
      faction has no cinematic, so its token is pinged straight away.
    - Players only see a ping where their characters can see.
    - If the turn has already moved on before the cinematic ends, there is
      no ping for the skipped turn.

THE KILL PICTURES  (placeholders are included; replace them with your art)

  killed-stamp.gif   The KILLED picture that lands on a killed enemy. It is
                     shown exactly as it is: a GIF (animated or not) or a
                     PNG, any width and height; the included one is an
                     animated GIF, 1000 x 460, that flickers and glitches
                     in a loop. It is drawn about a third of the screen
                     wide, tilted a little (-7 degrees, leaning left; set
                     "Kill stamp tilt" to 0 for none) and slammed down. A GIF
                     can only be fully see-through or fully solid, so give
                     it hard edges. If you empty the setting, or the file
                     is missing, nothing is shown in its place.
                     If your file has another name, point the "Kill: KILLED
                     picture" setting at it.

  blood-1.png        Four blood textures: transparent PNGs, square works well
  blood-2.png        (the included ones are 1024 x 1024). Nine splatters are
  blood-3.png        thrown onto the screen just before the picture lands,
  blood-4.png        and each one picks one of the four at random, at a
                     random turn and size, mirrored half of the time. The
                     first, big one under the picture uses the first texture
                     that is set. The included ones are: 1 a big splat with
                     flung droplets, 2 a spray of droplets, 3 a pool with
                     round splashes, 4 a smear with a fine mist. They are
                     drawn in dark red; yours can be any color.
                     Leave a texture's setting empty to use fewer than four
                     (leave all four empty for no blood). A texture whose
                     file is missing is simply left out.

  Black edges around the blood: some blood pictures hold a thin dark fringe
  around each splat, or a nearly invisible dark box around the whole picture,
  that looks fine in an image viewer but shows as a black border over the game.
  The setting "Kill: clean dark edges off the blood" (on by default, in your own
  settings) cleans that up when the pictures load: the barely visible pixels are
  removed, the partly see-through edge takes the color of the solid blood next
  to it, and a picture with no transparency at all on a black background has
  the black made see-through. It works on a copy in memory; your files are never
  changed. Turn it off to see the pictures exactly as they are.

  Sliders: "Kill stamp size", "Kill stamp tilt" and "Kill blood size".

KILL CINEMATICS (settings: "Kill cinematics", "Kill cinematic length")

  The moment an enemy is defeated, killed or removed, they get a short
  cinematic of their own: their faction's look, the color drained out of it,
  their name struck through, blood splattered across the screen, and your KILLED picture
  stamped across it with a thud.
    - What counts: the defeated box ticked on the combat tracker, the "dead"
      status put on their token, their token deleted from the scene, or them
      taken out of the combat tracker. It plays on everyone's screen at once,
      interrupting whatever is showing.
    - If several fall together (an area attack), they play one after another.
      Pressing Next Turn doesn't cut in: the next turn's cinematic waits
      until they've finished.
    - Each enemy gets one. Marking them defeated and then deleting the token
      afterwards doesn't play it twice. If you bring them back and they fall
      again, it plays again.
    - Who: enemies only (hostile tokens). Friends and player characters never
      get one. Enemies with no faction set are skipped. Enemies you've hidden
      are not shown to players. It only happens while a fight is running.
    - The blood comes from the four blood pictures above; every kill throws
      it a little differently. With "Lite" mode on there is less of it.
    - Click a kill cinematic to skip it.
    - The thud is "Kill sound" in the settings (it uses the Angel's stamp
      sound by default; replace it or empty it for silence).
    - Each player can turn this off for themselves.

Fonts are bundled in the fonts folder (see LICENSES.txt there).

The Angel's background: it has no band, video or borders. The only thing
behind it is a dark, tinted wash over the whole screen (deep red at the
higher grades). Turn off "Angel: darken the screen behind" to remove that too.
